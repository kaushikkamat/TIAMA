Where the name comes from

北海道にインスパイアされて。Inspired by Hokkaido.

Tiama's "Bokkaido Milk" takes its name from Hokkaido (北海道) — Japan's real northernmost island, and the place where most of the country's milk actually comes from. Cooler, greener, and a lot less crowded than the rest of Japan.

Cool by design

Hokkaido's summers rarely turn brutal. That matters for dairy cows — heat stresses them out and thins their milk. Cooler pastures mean calmer cows and richer, creamier milk.

Room to roam

Wide open grassland means cows here graze on real grass for more of the year than almost anywhere else in Japan, instead of living on feed alone.

A dairy tradition

Hokkaido has been Japan's dairy heartland for over a century, and today the island produces roughly half of all the milk in the country. Tiama is built on that reputation.

Quick questions

Is this a medicated soap?

No — Tiama is a gentle everyday cleansing bar, not a treatment product. Think daily ritual, not remedy.

Why start with a tiny 50 g bar?

So you can try Tiama for about the price of a cup of chai before deciding whether to commit to a full-size bar.

What's actually in a milk soap?

A gentle cleansing base carrying milk fats and glycerin — that's what gives Tiama its soft after-feel instead of the usual tight, dry finish.

Where can I buy it?

Look for the Tiama shelf tag at your local general store or pharmacy — that's exactly how you found this page.

Is "Bokkaido" a real place?

No — it's Tiama's own name for the idea. It's inspired by Hokkaido (北海道), Japan's real dairy heartland, reimagined as a name of our own.
